      How it works

      An agent x tells us a story: the rooms it visited, in order. A sparse network of sensors kept its own record: beam detectors report that someone crossed them, and occupancy sensors report when a region becomes occupied and when it empties again. We combine the map, the story and the recordings into one automaton whose states are a position of x and how much of the story x has told, so checking a story takes time proportional to its length times the number of recordings times the size of the map. When the story fits we return a witness walk, which the demo animates; when it does not, we say which recording or which room breaks it. Problems 2 to 4 relax the question: the story and the sensors may cover different time intervals, the story may leave visits out (we find the shortest consistent story that contains it), or it may contain errors (we find the fewest edits that make it consistent).

      Everything runs in your browser: the engine is a JavaScript port of our Python package, checked against it on thousands of recorded cases, and "What the original 2010 applet says" re-runs the original Java code, ported line by line with its bugs.
